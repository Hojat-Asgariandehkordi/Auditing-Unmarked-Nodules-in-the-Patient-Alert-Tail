\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Reference standards for lung nodule detection are themselves uncertain. LIDC collected annotations in two phases, in which each of four readers first marked a scan independently and then reviewed the anonymized marks of the others without a forced consensus~\cite{armato2007variability,armato2011}. Agreement nevertheless remained incomplete, and reader sensitivity varies widely with the panel that defines truth~\cite{armato2009truth}. LUNA16 retained lesions accepted by at least three of the four readers as targets~\cite{setio2017}, and a subsequent reading of detector outputs identified further nodules accepted by all four new readers~\cite{jacobs2016cad}. Work on sparse and missing labels addresses the training side of this problem: DeepLesion is mined from clinical bookmarks~\cite{yan2018}, Lesion-Harvester and EnrichedDL complete missing annotations from additional labels or model outputs~\cite{cai2021,ma2024}, multi-reader methods model reader-specific labels or confusion matrices~\cite{tanno2019,wu2024}, and positive--unlabeled learning treats unmarked examples as a mixture of both classes~\cite{kiryo2017,bekker2018}; see~\cite{karimi2020noisy} for a survey of label noise in medical imaging. In contrast to these methods, we define omissions against a separate spatial reference, use one reader's marks to define omissions and alternative reference rules to define alternative negative pools, and count the distinct lesions an audit reaches, including lesions that no high-scoring proposal covers.

Our audit design builds on score-guided acquisition and corrected training. Multi-view and volumetric scores are standard for false-positive reduction in CT~\cite{setio2016views,setio2017}, and deep active learning has selected CT examples by uncertainty~\cite{liu2020active}. Two-phase sampling and active statistical inference use model predictions to direct labelling under a budget~\cite{chen2020sampling,zrnic2024active,li2025robust}; known inclusion probabilities permit unbiased estimation~\cite{horvitz1952}, augmented inverse-probability weighting with cross-fitting reduces its variance~\cite{robins1994,chernozhukov2018}, and prediction-powered inference is closely related~\cite{angelopoulos2023ppi,ppssl2025}. Unlike these approaches, our design uses the within-scan rank and the low-alert threshold to decide where to sample, and measures what paired weak marks add at a fixed budget. On the evaluation side, conformal and order-statistic thresholds assume that calibration scores are exchangeable with those of the target population~\cite{angelopoulos2023conformal}, recent work analyses the effect of noisy calibration labels~\cite{einbinder2024label,feldman2026corrupted}, label errors in test sets can change which model ranks first~\cite{northcutt2021labelerrors}, and validation guidelines emphasize the quality of the reference and the choice of endpoint~\cite{maierhein2024metrics}. We quantify a related but distinct effect for patient alerts: which scans count as negative determines which maxima set the threshold, even when every candidate score is unchanged.
