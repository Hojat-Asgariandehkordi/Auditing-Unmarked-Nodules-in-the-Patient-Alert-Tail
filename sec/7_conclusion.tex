\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have separated what an audit reveals from what a detector recovers and from where its alert threshold falls. A probability audit guided by scan rank and reader disagreement finds far more unmarked nodules per review than mark-stratified review, yet these labels did not raise omission recall at the locked threshold. With all scores fixed, the threshold itself moved by several ranks when only 14 consensus-positive patients were removed from the negative pool, and the sign of the method comparison changed. We therefore argue that, in low-alert evaluations of lesion detectors, the negative reference should be treated as a modelling decision whose effect is reported alongside the results.
